Chordify alternatives in 2026: five chord finders, compared honestly

Updated 11 November 2026 · Prices checked against each vendor's own site or app-store listing on that date

We make Chord Finder AI, one of the five tools below, so read this with that in mind. We've tried to be straight about where the others are better — several are, depending on what you need — and we'd rather you pick the right one than pick ours and leave.

Chordify is the tool most people meet first, and for a lot of them it's the right one: a huge catalogue of songs already processed, instant results, and paid plans that cost less than a coffee. People go looking for an alternative for a handful of specific reasons.

  • The free tier's daily cap. Two songs a day without an account, four with one, with ads. Fine for a casual look, tight if you're learning a set.
  • You want to isolate an instrument — hear just the bass, or kill the vocal so you can sing over it. That's a different kind of tool.
  • You want the key and BPM up front, and the three or four chords the song actually lives on, rather than a scrolling grid of every chord change.
  • You're working on your own recording — a demo, a rehearsal, a song that isn't on YouTube.
  • You want chords live from a microphone, in the room, while someone plays.
  • You want a human-checked chart for a famous song, not a machine's best guess.

1. Chord ai — the most complete phone app

iOS and Android only · Free with limits · Pro about $10.99/month (varies by region), $69.99/year, or $249 one-off lifetime

Chord ai does more things than any other tool here. It recognises chords from your phone's microphone in real time, from YouTube and SoundCloud links, and from audio files. It shows guitar, piano and ukulele diagrams, tracks beats and downbeats, separates stems (bass, vocals, drums, other) with WAV export, transcribes lyrics, and converts audio to MIDI. It claims over 500,000 users and sits at about 4.7 stars on both app stores, which matches our experience of it.

Better than Chordify at

  • Live detection from a microphone — nothing else here does this well
  • Stems, lyrics and MIDI in the same app
  • Works on your own files without a catalogue

Worse at

  • Phone only — no web or desktop version
  • The priciest monthly plan on this page
  • No catalogue: every song is processed on your phone

2. Moises — when the stems matter more than the chart

Web, desktop, iOS, Android · Free: 5 separations a month plus key and BPM · Premium $3.99/month on the web (30 uploads a month), Pro $9.99/month on the web (unlimited) · App-store prices run higher

Moises is a stem-separation tool first — it's the one musicians use to pull the vocal out of a track, slow a solo down without changing pitch, or practise with the bass turned off. Chord detection is in there, and it's decent, but it's a feature rather than the point. If you came to Chordify for the chords and stayed for wishing you could mute the guitar, Moises is what you're looking for.

Better than Chordify at

  • Isolating or muting any instrument
  • Pitch and speed tools, metronome, lyrics
  • Runs everywhere, including desktop

Worse at

  • Chords are secondary; no distilled progression
  • Monthly upload caps on the free and Premium tiers
  • Not built around learning a song's chords

3. Yalp — cheap, web-based, with lessons

Web · Free with limits (10-song playlist, no transpose, no uploads, no loops) · Premium $3.49/month or $3/month billed yearly · Premium+ $7.95/month or $6.81/month billed yearly adds lessons

Yalp is the closest thing to Chordify-but-different: chords from YouTube links, transposition, loops and separated instrument tracks on the paid plans, and a tuner. Its distinguishing feature is lessons on the Premium+ tier. The free tier is noticeably more restricted than Chordify's — no transposing, no uploads — so it's really a paid tool with a preview.

Better than Chordify at

  • Separated instrument tracks on paid plans
  • Built-in lessons (Premium+)
  • MIDI download

Worse at

  • Free tier is tighter than Chordify's
  • Web only — no apps
  • Smaller community and catalogue

4. Chord Finder AI — key, BPM and the main progression, on the web

Web, works on any device with no install · Free: 1 full analysis of any song you choose, no card, plus four pre-analyzed songs to play along with · Pro $12/month or $120/year (15 analyses a month) · Premium $29/month or $290/year (40 a month)

This is ours. You paste a YouTube link or upload an audio file, wait a minute or two, and get the song's key, BPM and time signature, the chords synced to the music as it plays, and — the part we care most about — the main repeating progression pulled out on its own, so a song with 40 chord changes reads as "G – D – Em – C" if that's what it really is. Guitar and piano diagrams are free for everyone. There's an ear-training mode that hides the answers so you guess the key and chords first, and practice streaks if that sort of thing keeps you going. There are no ads.

We'll be plain about the trade-offs. There is no catalogue: every song is analyzed when you ask for it, which is why it takes a minute, and why the paid plans are per-analysis rather than unlimited. Songs are analyzed up to five minutes (longer tracks get their first five). Chords come back as triads — no slash chords or inversions, which is true of everything on this page but we'd rather say it. And there's no stem separation; if you need that, use Moises.

Better than Chordify at

  • Key, BPM and time signature stated up front
  • The main progression distilled from the full chord list
  • Ear training built into the player; no ads

Worse at

  • Costs more per song — Chordify's Basic plan is unlimited for $2/month
  • Waits a minute or two per song; no instant catalogue
  • No stems, no native app, 5-minute song limit

5. Ultimate Guitar — the non-AI option

Web, iOS, Android · Free with ads · Pro subscription

Not a chord finder at all: Ultimate Guitar is a library of charts and tabs written and corrected by people. For a famous song that's usually a better chart than any algorithm will give you, with the right voicings and a capo suggestion. For anything obscure, a cover, a live version or your own recording, it has nothing. The two kinds of tool complement each other more than they compete.

Which one should you actually pick?

Lots of songs, cheaply: stay on Chordify, or upgrade to its Basic plan. Nothing here beats $2 a month for unlimited.

On a phone, want everything in one app: Chord ai.

Need to mute or isolate an instrument: Moises.

Want a cheap web tool with lessons: Yalp Premium+.

Want the key, BPM and the chords that matter for one specific song, and to train your ear on it: Chord Finder AI. Try the free song first — if it gets your song right, you'll know in two minutes.

Famous song, want it played right: Ultimate Guitar.

One thing none of them will tell you

Every tool on this page, ours included, runs automatic chord recognition, and automatic chord recognition simplifies. A G/B comes back as G, or occasionally as a stray B minor. A Cmaj7 is usually just C. Dense jazz voicings get flattened into something a beginner could play, which is either a feature or a bug depending on who you are. Songs that change key halfway through can throw any of them. On pop, rock, folk, worship and R&B with clear harmony they're all broadly usable; the differences are in what each shows you and what it costs. The honest test is to take the one song you most want to learn and run it through two or three free tiers before paying for anything.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free alternative to Chordify?

For volume, nothing free beats Chordify's own four songs a day. Chord ai's free tier is the most capable on a phone, Moises gives you five separations a month plus key and BPM, and Chord Finder AI gives one full analysis of any song you choose plus four pre-analyzed songs. It depends on whether you want many songs or a closer look at one.

Is there a Chordify alternative that works on my own recordings?

Yes — Chord ai, Moises, Yalp (paid) and Chord Finder AI all accept uploaded audio, so they work on demos, rehearsal recordings and unreleased songs. Chordify accepts uploads too; its real strength is the catalogue.

Which chord finder is the most accurate?

They all simplify in the same ways (see above). None of them is reliably better across every genre, so test with the song you care about.

Does Chord Finder AI have a mobile app?

No. It's a web app that works in any phone or desktop browser with nothing to install. If you specifically want a native app, Chord ai or Chordify.

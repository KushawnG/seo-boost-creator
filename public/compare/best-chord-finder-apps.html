The best chord finder apps in 2026, compared honestly

Updated 11 November 2026 · Prices checked against each vendor's own site or app-store listing on that date

We make one of these (Chord Finder AI). Rather than rank ourselves first, we've organised this by what each tool is genuinely best at, said plainly where others beat us, and put the limits that all of them share — ours included — in their own section. Pick by your problem, not by our logo.

"Chord finder" covers two different kinds of software. Most of these listen to audio and work the chords out with a machine-learning model; one of them is a library of charts written by people. Both are useful, for different songs. Here is what each does well, what it costs, and what it won't do.

Best catalogue and lowest price: Chordify

Web, iOS, Android · Free: 2 songs a day without an account, 4 with one, with ads · Basic $2/month billed $24/year (unlimited songs) · Premium $3.50/month billed $42/year (transpose, capo, tempo, loops and more) · a Premium Plus tier above that

Chordify has already processed an enormous number of songs, so for anything well known the chords are there instantly, laid out on a scrolling grid in time with the video. Guitar, piano and ukulele diagrams. The free tier's daily cap and ads are the main complaint, and both disappear for two dollars a month. If you learn a lot of covers and want to spend almost nothing, start here.

Weak spots: no live microphone detection, no stems outside the top-tier Toolkit, and it gives you every chord change rather than the handful the song is built on.

Best on a phone, and the only good live option: Chord ai

iOS, Android · Free with limits · Pro about $10.99/month (varies by region), $69.99/year, or $249 lifetime

Chord ai listens. Point your phone at a speaker, a record, a bandmate, and it shows the chords as they happen — nothing else here does this well. It also takes YouTube and SoundCloud links and audio files, tracks beats and downbeats, separates stems with WAV export, transcribes lyrics and converts audio to MIDI. It claims over 500,000 users at around 4.7 stars on both stores. The most complete single app on this page.

Weak spots: no web or desktop version at all, and the priciest monthly plan here. If you only want the chords and you're at a laptop, it isn't the one.

Best for isolating instruments: Moises

Web, desktop, iOS, Android · Free: 5 separations a month plus key and BPM · Premium $3.99/month on the web (30 uploads a month) · Pro $9.99/month on the web (unlimited) · App-store prices run higher

Moises is the tool for hearing just the bass line, muting the vocal to sing over the track, or slowing a solo down without changing its pitch. Chord detection, key and BPM are in there and perfectly usable, but it's a stem separator with chords attached rather than the reverse. If "I wish I could turn the guitar off" is your real problem, this is the answer.

Weak spots: chords are a secondary feature; monthly upload caps on the free and Premium tiers.

Best cheap web tool with lessons: Yalp

Web · Free with limits (10-song playlist, no transpose, no uploads, no loops) · Premium $3.49/month or $3/month billed yearly · Premium+ $7.95/month or $6.81/month billed yearly adds lessons

Yalp pulls chords from YouTube links and, on paid plans, from uploaded MP3s with separated instrument tracks, transposition, loops, a tuner and MIDI download. Premium+ adds lessons, which none of the others offer. The free tier is restrictive enough that it's best thought of as a trial.

Weak spots: web only, a smaller catalogue and community than Chordify, and a tight free tier.

You paste a YouTube link or upload a file and, a minute or two later, get the song's key, BPM and time signature stated up front; the chords synced to the music as it plays, with guitar and piano diagrams for every one; and the main repeating progression pulled out on its own, so a song with forty changes reads as the four chords it actually lives on. Best human-written charts: Ultimate Guitar

Web, iOS, Android · Free with ads · Pro subscription

Not an AI tool. Ultimate Guitar is the biggest library of charts and tabs written and corrected by people, and for a famous song that's usually a better chart than any algorithm gives you: the right voicings, a capo suggestion, a strumming pattern. For anything obscure, a live version, a cover or your own recording it has nothing, which is exactly the gap the other five fill. Many players use one of each.

What none of them do well

Every automatic chord finder on this page — including ours — simplifies in the same ways, and it's worth knowing before you pay for any of them:

  • Slash chords and inversions come back as the root triad. A G/B is shown as G, or occasionally as a stray B minor where the bass note confused the model.
  • Sevenths and extensions are often flattened: Cmaj7 and C9 tend to read as C. Fine for strumming along, wrong for a jazz voicing.
  • Dense jazz and heavily layered productions get reduced to something a beginner could play, which is a feature or a bug depending on who you are.
  • Key changes mid-song can throw any of them, and a song that sits between a major key and its relative minor is sometimes labelled with the other one.
  • Passing chords that last one beat are either dropped or flicker in and out.

On pop, rock, folk, worship and R&B with clear harmony, all of them are usable. The honest test is to run the one song you most want to learn through two or three free tiers and see which one gets it right.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best app to find the chords of a song?

There isn't one best app. Chordify for catalogue and price; Chord ai for a phone and live detection; Moises for isolating instruments; Yalp for a cheap web tool with lessons; Chord Finder AI for one song's key, BPM and main progression with ear training; Ultimate Guitar for human charts of famous songs.

Is there a free app that tells you the chords of any song?

All of these have free tiers — see the table above. The most generous for volume is Chordify's four songs a day with an account.

How accurate are AI chord finders?

Good enough to learn most pop, rock, folk, worship and R&B from; worse on jazz, dense productions and key changes. They all simplify slash chords and extensions. Treat the output as a strong first draft.

What app tells you the key and BPM of a song?

Chord Finder AI puts key, BPM and time signature first for any link or file; Moises detects key and BPM on its free tier; Chord ai and Chordify show tempo and key alongside the chords. Our chord library also lists the key, BPM and main progression of songs that have already been analyzed, free to read.

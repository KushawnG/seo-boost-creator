Chordify vs Chord ai: which chord app should you use in 2026?

Updated 11 November 2026 · Prices checked against each vendor's own site or app-store listing on that date

We make Chord Finder AI, a competitor to both. We've kept this to what each app verifiably does and costs, and where we mention our own tool it's in a clearly separate section at the end. If you only read one line: Chordify is cheaper and runs on the web; Chord ai does more on a phone.

These are the two names that come up first when someone asks how to get the chords for a song, and they're built on different ideas. Chordify is a catalogue: millions of songs already processed, so results are instant and cheap. Chord ai is an instrument: it listens — to a file, a link, or the room — and works it out on the spot.

Where Chordify wins

Price. It isn't close. Two dollars a month for unlimited songs, three-fifty for the full set of practice tools, versus roughly eleven for Chord ai Pro. If you'll use it every week for a year, Chordify Premium costs you $42 and Chord ai Pro costs you $69.99 — or $249 if you'd rather own it outright.

The web. Chordify works on a laptop with a big screen, which matters when you're sitting at a piano or want the chart on a music stand. Chord ai is phone and tablet only.

Speed. If the song is in the catalogue — and for anything well known it is — the chords are there before you've finished typing the title.

Where Chord ai wins

Listening to the room. Point the phone at a speaker, a record player, a bandmate, and it tells you what's being played as it's played. Nothing else at this price does this well. For jamming, teaching and transcribing from vinyl it's a different category of tool.

Everything in one app. Stems, so you can hear just the bass line you're trying to learn. Lyrics transcribed from the audio. Downbeat tracking. Export to MIDI. Chordify sells some of this as Toolkit extras on its top tier; Chord ai has had it in the main app for years.

Your own recordings. Both accept uploads, but Chord ai's whole design assumes the song isn't in any catalogue. If most of what you play is demos, rehearsal takes or unreleased material, that's the right assumption.

What they share, and what neither will tell you

Both run automatic chord recognition, and both simplify in the same ways. Slash chords come back as their root triad — a G/B becomes G, or sometimes a stray B minor. Sevenths and extended voicings are usually flattened to plain major and minor. A song that changes key can confuse either. On pop, rock, folk, worship and R&B with clear harmony, both are broadly usable and the difference comes down to what each shows you and what it costs. For a famous song, a human-written chart on Ultimate Guitar will often have better voicings than either.

The verdict

Pick Chordify if you mostly play well-known songs, you want to use it on a laptop, or you want to spend as little as possible. For most people learning covers, this is the answer.

Pick Chord ai if you live on your phone, you want chords live from a microphone, or you want stems, lyrics and MIDI without juggling apps. Worth the price if you'd actually use those things; not if you only want the chords.

Frequently asked questions

Is Chord ai better than Chordify?

They're good at different things. Chord ai is the more capable app — live detection, stems, lyrics, MIDI — but it's phone-only and its Pro plan costs about three times Chordify Premium. Chordify is cheaper, works on the web, and has a huge catalogue.

Which is cheaper?

Chordify, by a wide margin: $2–$3.50 a month billed yearly, versus about $10.99 a month, $69.99 a year or $249 lifetime for Chord ai Pro. Both have free tiers.

Can Chordify detect chords from a microphone?

Not in the way Chord ai does. Live, real-time recognition from the phone's microphone is Chord ai's signature feature; Chordify works from its catalogue, YouTube links and uploads.

Is there a web-based alternative to both?

Chordify itself is on the web. For a web tool focused on one song's key, BPM and main progression with ear training, Chord Finder AI; for a cheap web tool with lessons, Yalp; for stems, Moises. We compare all of them in the best chord finder apps of 2026.
